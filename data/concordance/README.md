Each file starts with `_meta`, a header object that describes the file (see [`data/README.md`](../README.md)); field definitions are in `_meta.fields`, and `eras.json` adds `_meta.yearSpace` (the year numbering) and, on each line, `line` (the succession followed). The data follows under the key or keys in the Payload column, one entry per line.

## Lookup

- Years in `y`, `s`, `since`, `until` and `before` are astronomical.
- `eras`: the lines `zh`, `ja`, `vi` and `xx` are year tables. The era in force in a year is the last entry with `y` not greater than that year (and, in `vi` and `xx`, with `m` not greater than the month). The ordinal year counts from `s` when present, otherwise from `y`; in `vi` and `xx` `y` is the year of the era's first year, and the chronicle counts the year of a mid-year change as the new era's first year. `m` is the lunar month in which the era takes effect when it is not the first. An entry with `gap: true` means no era is in use from that year. A year outside a line's range has no era. In `zh`, `forms` are other spellings an edition may print and `errata` are misprints that a reader's text corrects; both name the same era as `nt`. The lines `ko`, `ja-other` and `zh-other` carry no years: they name the reigns, emperors and eras that the records print. `forms` and `errata` must each occur in some record's `text_orig` or in the text of an edition. `n`, `nt` and `ja` are the name in Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and Japanese (`ja` only where it differs from `nt`); `rom` is the Latin-script name that fr, es and it print and en prints before the line's `unit` word (reign or era). A record's `supplied` token `era=<key>` names the entry whose `k`, or else whose lowercased accent-free `rom`, is the key; `k` is written only where another name has the same romanisation.
- `xingguan`: keys are simplified-script names; unmatched names are looked up in `alias`; `alias_rom` gives the romanization of an alias read differently from its key. `alias_west` lists phrases a Latin- or Greek-script source writes for the star, such as `cœur du lion` for Regulus. Keys with a parenthesised qualifier name distinct asterisms and are matched whole. `modern` holds the modern identification; `modern.proper` is a star's IAU proper name by locale, `en` as the IAU writes it and `fr`/`es`/`it`/`ja` where the locale writes it otherwise, and a note slot may hold the key of a star that has `modern.proper`. `modern.bayer` and `member_bayer` (one per `members` place, `null` where none) give the Bayer or Flamsteed designation, as `α Leo` or `31 Leo`. `hant` is the traditional-script form; `ja` is `hant` in shinjitai.
- `gazetteer`: `name` holds the historical names by locale, `modern` the modern names where they differ, `aliases` other historical names. `colloquial` holds the short names a period's press used (华府 for Washington), an array per language; the checks decide which genres and years may print them. Keys sharing coordinates are split by `since` and `until`, which do not overlap. `name.en` is the Latin-script name.
- `regions`: an anchor with `since` or `before` applies only from or before that year.
- `civilizations`: each entry maps the seven locale tags to a display name.
- `organizations`: `latin` is the organization's own name; `abbr` lists printed abbreviations, and an entry with `abbr` carries `provenance`.
- `calendars`: `epoch` is the astronomical Julian year in which year 1 begins. `gender` marks the French, Spanish and Italian names that are feminine (`"f"`), so a note can give them the right article; a locale left out is masculine. `note` is the sentence that explains the era, in all seven locales, shown as a hover card on the era's name in a record's text. `names` lists the era's proper names as the record texts print them, per locale and under `orig` for the source text (Latin script matched case-insensitively; a Chinese or Japanese name is skipped when a neighbouring character makes it part of another word, as in 这回历法); `names_after_year` lists names that are also a person's name (Alexander, Diocletian), matched only right after a year numeral and *of*, *de*, *di* or *d’*. `months[].n` is the month's position, ascending (a calendar lists all twelve months of a fixed year, or only the months a record names); `forms` are the spellings sources print, matched lower-cased; `julian` is the corresponding Julian month for fixed solar calendars; `gloss` is the sentence a `glossary_term` note of sub-case `month` prints to explain the month name. `variants` are Chinese spellings the corpus no longer uses, checked by the `name-variant-zh` rule.
- `persons`: `latin` is the scholarly transliteration; `variants` as in `calendars`.
- `terms`: `kind` is `unit`, `term`, `word` or `lunar`; `domain` is `astronomy`, `astrology`, `calendar` or `timekeeping` on every entry but a lunar feature, and an entry without one is an error. A word of another field has no entry: a phrasing word (是年, *that year*) is written out in the note, and a fixed rendering of another science (*evenredig* 成正比, Réaumur 列氏度) has no entry either. For a unit or term `gloss` is the sentence a note prints after the word it explains, for a word or a named lunar feature it is the word or name itself in seven locales. An entry with `forms` (a case-insensitive pattern of the spellings base texts print) has a fixed Chinese rendering, which the `term-pairs-zh` rule checks: a record whose `text_orig` matches `forms` more often than its zh-Hans matches `zh` (or, without `zh`, the zh-Hans gloss) is reported. `names_after_number` lists, per locale and under `orig`, the unit words (指, *digits*, chi) the page marks, together with the numeral before them, with the unit's `gloss` as a hover card. A unit's `gloss` and a calendar's `note` open with the word they define ("The chi is ...", 尺为……单位), since the card sits on that word.
- `bodies`: each entry maps the seven locale tags to the name the translations print; `kind` is `moon`, `planet` or `planets` (the five together); `alias` lists the names the sources write for the body (熒惑, 荧惑, 火 for Mars); `token` is the value of a `supplied` token `subject=<token>`. A note slot that would hold the same seven names holds the key instead, as it does for an entry of `persons` or a `word` of `terms`. The books, articles and editions a note cites are entries of [`data/records/sources.json`](../records/sources.json) (`form` there decides their marks).
